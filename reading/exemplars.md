# Exemplars

The weekly readings are listed in `PROGRAM.md`. This file collects them, plus books and a longer list for after week 12, grouped by what each one teaches. Verify links before relying on them; blog URLs move.

## Books on writing (read one per quarter)

- Joseph Williams and Joseph Bizup, *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace*. The source of characters-as-subjects, actions-as-verbs and old-before-new. The most useful book on sentences.
- Steven Pinker, *The Sense of Style* (2014). The curse of knowledge, and why "classic style" (showing the reader something you both can see) suits explanation.
- William Zinsser, *On Writing Well*. Clutter, and how to cut it.
- Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle*. Conclusion first; grouping arguments. Written for consultants; essential for white papers.
- Claus Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization* (free online). Figures.
- Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*. Figures, with strong opinions.
- Justin Zobel, *Writing for Computer Science*. Papers, end to end, including experiments and ethics.

## Short guides (one sitting each)

- George Gopen and Judith Swan, *The Science of Scientific Writing* (American Scientist, 1990).
- Simon Peyton Jones, *How to Write a Great Research Paper* (talk and slides).
- Paul Halmos, *How to Write Mathematics* (1970).
- Donald Knuth, Tracy Larrabee and Paul Roberts, *Mathematical Writing* (1989).
- Gernot Heiser, *Systems Benchmarking Crimes*.
- Roy Levin and David Redell, *How (and How Not) to Write a Good Systems Paper* (1983).
- Zachary Lipton and Jacob Steinhardt, *Troubling Trends in Machine Learning Scholarship* (2018).
- Rishabh Agarwal et al., *Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice* (NeurIPS 2021). On reporting reinforcement learning results.

## By topic: read for the moves

**GPUs and kernels**
- Simon Boehm, *How to Optimize a CUDA Matmul Kernel for cuBLAS-like Performance: a Worklog*. The worklog form.
- Horace He, *Making Deep Learning Go Brrrr From First Principles*. Explaining performance regimes to practitioners.
- Tri Dao et al., *FlashAttention* (NeurIPS 2022). An argument from memory traffic, made in a paper.
- Samuel Williams, Andrew Waterman and David Patterson, *Roofline* (CACM 2009). A model explained so well that it became standard.

**Training**
- Andrej Karpathy, *A Recipe for Training Neural Networks* (blog, 2019). Opinion grounded in practice.
- Jared Kaplan et al., *Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models* (2020). Presenting empirical laws with figures.
- The Hugging Face *Ultra-Scale Playbook* (2025). Long-form systems explanation for practitioners.

**Inference**
- Woosuk Kwon et al., *Efficient Memory Management for Large Language Model Serving with PagedAttention* (SOSP 2023). A systems paper built on one clear analogy.

**Reinforcement learning**
- Lilian Weng, *Policy Gradient Algorithms* (blog, 2018). Mathematics in a blog.
- John Schulman et al., *Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms* (2017). A short paper with one idea.
- Peter Henderson et al., *Deep Reinforcement Learning that Matters* (AAAI 2018). Evidence standards.

**Explainers**
- Jay Alammar, *The Illustrated Transformer*. Figures carrying a mechanism.
- Chris Olah, *Understanding LSTM Networks* <!-- twlint: ignore acronym -->. Building from what the reader knows.
- Distill.pub articles (2016–2021). Interactive explanation at its best.

**Systems narratives**
- Google, *Site Reliability Engineering*, chapter 15 (postmortem culture).
- Dan Luu, `post-mortems` (GitHub). A catalogue of real incidents.
