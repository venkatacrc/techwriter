# Explainer rubric

| Row | Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Reader and purpose | No identifiable starting point | Starting point assumed too high or too low | Starts from what the reader knows | Starts from what the reader knows and names the misconception it corrects |
| R2 | Concept | Several concepts, none finished | One concept, not stated | One concept, stated at the end | One concept, stated early and restated at the end |
| R3 | Progression | Leaps the reader cannot follow | Some leaps | One new idea per paragraph, mostly | Each step depends only on earlier steps; nothing is used before it is introduced |
| R4 | Worked example | None | Toy example disconnected from the concept | Concrete example with real numbers | Example with real numbers that the reader can check, exercising the hardest step |
| R5 | Sentences and words | Hard to read aloud | Undefined terms, long sentences | Mostly clear | Every term defined at first use; plain and concrete throughout |
| R6 | Figures | None where the mechanism is spatial | Decorative | Each figure shows one step | The hardest step to picture has a figure that makes it obvious |
| R7 | Limits of the intuition | Simplification presented as the whole truth | Limits mentioned in passing | Limits stated | Limits stated, with what the expert knows that the explanation omits |
